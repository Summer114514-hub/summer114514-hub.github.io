About

I'm Zilin He, a third-year mathematics student at ShanghaiTech University on the financial mathematics track, spending Fall 2026 at UC Berkeley. I grew up in Hubei.

What I'm aiming at is financial engineering: the place where stochastic control, statistics and markets meet, and where a model has to survive contact with data that will not cooperate. The question I keep coming back to is the gap between the model an investor writes down and the parameters the data can actually pin down. On the empirical side that shows up as estimation error getting amplified by optimisation; on the theory side as robust control with an uncertain drift. Most of what is on this site is an attempt to look at that gap from different angles. Two of the projects are about football rather than finance; they are here because they turned out to be the same kind of problem, a fixed rule or a scoring process evaluated over every state it might face, and because pricing a red card in expected points is the same move as pricing a risk in expected utility.

How I work: I'd rather have one number I can defend than ten I can't. I write the design and the checks first, then the model. Each project page identifies the work I completed myself. Where I used AI tools to produce implementation code, I describe that assistance and how I checked the output. For what it's worth, I test as an INTJ, which matches how I like to work: a plan first, alone for long stretches, then a short argument with someone who disagrees.

After Berkeley: a first quantitative internship in Shanghai in early 2027, a semester of research in financial mathematics at ShanghaiTech, and graduate study in financial engineering from 2028.

Outside work: track and field, long jump and the 4×100 m relay; and lately, a daily habit of reading the financial and political news properly rather than skimming it.
