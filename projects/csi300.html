Price–volume signals in CSI 300 stock selection, and how much leaderboard advantage survives

Question

Two questions, one empirical and one about the competition itself. First: on the CSI 300, do price–volume signals (trade-weighted-price deviation, similar-stock co-movement) improve a LightGBM cross-sectional baseline out of sample? Second: when 85 teams optimise against a public leaderboard, how much of the public advantage is real and how much is selection on noise?

Setup

Organiser data for 2017–2022 (three tables). Six-year walk-forward evaluation with the number of models and the random seeds held equal between the baseline and the augmented model, so that any RankIC difference cannot be explained by extra tuning. Portfolio returns are computed through the official constrained optimiser; confidence intervals use 21-day block resampling.

Results

  • Price–volume signals give small but consistent RankIC gains (+0.0054 and +0.0025); the seed-only control moves by at most 0.0006.
  • The gain in portfolio information ratio is not significant once the 95% interval is drawn.
  • Across all 85 teams, regressing private score on public score gives a slope of 0.28; the number of submissions correlates 0.60 with public rank and only 0.14 with private rank. Most of the public leaderboard is selection, not skill.

What I did myself

The experimental design and the evaluation protocol (equal model counts, placebo seeds, block resampling). I wrote an independent verification script that recomputes factor RankIC, coverage, and the leaderboard regressions from the raw tables, and used it to check every number in the draft. I wrote the paper. The model training code was produced with AI tools; it was reproduced on Windows and Linux and the 58 submitted models match bit-for-bit.

Status and limits

Manuscript in preparation for a Chinese CSCD journal. The private test period is not disclosed by the organiser, so the paper describes it as an unknown future window. I have not yet rewritten the model code by hand; that is on my list before the paper is submitted.
